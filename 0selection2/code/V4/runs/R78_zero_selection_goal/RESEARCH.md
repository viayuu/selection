## 3. What A Zero Pick Does And Does Not Mean

Let `Y=argmin_j C_j` under the fixed native tie rule, and let the available
predictive information be `X`. Natural Top1 and cost have different Bayes rules:

\[
 a_{acc}(x)=\arg\max_j P(Y=j\mid X=x),\qquad
 a_{risk}(x)=\arg\min_j E[r_j\mid X=x],\quad
 r_j=(C_j-C_*)/C_*,\ C_*=\min_k C_k.
\]

These definitions assume finite positive routing costs and the current valid
candidate mask. Invalid/unavailable methods are not zero-pick failures.

- Positive marginal winner probability does not imply a method maximizes the
  conditional winner probability anywhere accessible to the model.
- Even a frequent conditional winner can have a poor expected cost because its
  losses are much larger than its wins. Synthetic illustration, not R76 data:
  B saves 1 versus A in 60% of indistinguishable cases but loses 10 in 40%.
  Top1 favors B; expected cost favors A by 3.4. Always choosing A can be rational.
- `E[C_j|X]` and `E[(C_j-C_*)/C_*|X]` also differ because normalization weights
  instances. Mean cost, relative regret, and cost-vs-SBS must stay separate.
- Matching mean predicted probability to TRAIN class frequency does not require
  matching argmax frequency. Intercept fitting may improve NLL while leaving
  all zero-pick counts unchanged. That is not a fitting contradiction.
- More frequent selection without higher precision or beneficial cost changes
  is not evidence of recovered instance discrimination.

### Ranking Invariances

With a unique highest utility `u_k` and runner-up `u_(2)`, the stated maximin
map gives `z_k=u_k-u_(2)>0` and `z_i=u_i-u_k<0` for all other valid candidates.
The full ranking is the utility ranking, with consistent ties.

Consequently, global positive temperature leaves greedy Top1 unchanged.
More specifically, **positive candidate-specific scaling of these unshifted
maximin scores alone also cannot replace the winner**: signs do not change.
This second conclusion does not extend to arbitrary centered logits or to
method-dependent scaling applied upstream to raw utilities. Within positive
diagonal affine transformations of these unshifted maximin scores, changing
the winner requires an additive component. Post-maximin placement is the
chosen intervention, not a consequence of the proof. Winner preservation for
fixed utilities says nothing about gradients or learning through maximin.

Post-maximin `z+b` is NOT numerically equivalent to rebuilding
`min_j[(u_i+b_i)-(u_j+b_j)]`. Choose and document one location; R78-A explicitly
uses the former. The original pairwise scores remain untouched.

## 4. R78-A: Restricted Affine Mechanism Screen

For each problem `t` with its existing `K_t` valid candidate identities, use

\[
 s_{tj}(x)=z_{tj}(x)+b_{tj},\quad \sum_jb_{tj}=0,\qquad
 \hat b_t=\arg\min_b\left\{
 \frac1{N_t}\sum_{x\in TRAIN_t}-\log\operatorname{softmax}(z(x)+b)_{Y(x)}
 +\frac1{2N_t}\sum_j b_{tj}^2\right\}.
\]

This is `K_t-1` free parameters, equivalent to summed CE with a unit-variance
zero-centered Gaussian prior on the sum-zero subspace. The penalty is a fixed
design choice in the frozen score units, not a paper-derived optimal coefficient.
Its normalization is explicit to prevent changing its meaning between summed
and mean losses.
The objective is convex for fixed scores and masks; the penalty makes the
parameter solution unique on the constrained subspace. Numerical convergence
still needs an actual fitting report from the execution owner. Exhausting the
predeclared budget without convergence makes the fit inconclusive, not negative.

At the constrained optimum, `mean(p_j)-TRAIN_frequency_j = -b_j/N_t`.
This gives a low-cost marginal diagnostic: an exactly zero vector of marginal
residuals at identity makes identity the optimum of this convex fit, even if
argmax never picks some j. Small residuals alone do not bound rare-method
parameter changes or changes in discrete decisions.
Shrinkage means probability matching is approximate. Neither matching nor
mismatching marginals establishes conditional calibration or recoverable
instance-level accuracy.

| ID | Paper / venue | Full-text evidence inspected | R78 implication and limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | Guo et al., ICML 2017 | Sec.4.2, Eq.8-9; Sec.5 | Validation-NLL affine/vector calibration is defined; positive temperature preserves decisions. Our TRAIN-fit intercept-only use is an adaptation, not their accuracy guarantee. |
| P2 | Kull et al., NeurIPS 2019 | Sec.3; Sec.4 fitting/ODIR, pp.5-6; Sec.5 | Log-probability affine calibration and explicit intercept regularization support capacity control. Their cross-validated calibration results do not guarantee regret gains or justify a full matrix here. |
| P3 | Menon et al., ICLR 2021 | Conference version Sec.2-5, Eq.2,7-10 | Subtract log-prior post-hoc; add log-prior inside training CE. Guarantees concern balanced error, not our natural-frequency Top1/regret. Do not apply both corrections blindly. |
| P4 | Ren et al., NeurIPS 2020 | Sec.3.1, Eq.2-4; Sec.3.2 | Balanced Softmax addresses a specified training/evaluation label-distribution mismatch. R78 has not established that mismatch or a uniform target prior. |
| P5 | Kang et al., ICLR 2020 | Sec.4 and Sec.5.2-5.3 | Frozen representations with altered classifiers can help long-tail recognition. Their class-balanced cRT and norm scaling are not natural-frequency cost guarantees. We borrow separation, not rebalancing. |
| P6 | Lipton et al., ICML 2018 | Sec.1-3, label-shift definition and BBSE assumptions | Prior correction assumes invariant class-conditionals and identifiable shift. Changed solver pools/winner frequencies do not establish this. No TEST-distribution fitting is proposed. |
| P7 | Avila Pires et al., ICML 2013 | Sec.2-2.1, Eq.1-3 and theorem scope | Cost-sensitive classification targets conditional expected action cost; surrogate guarantees depend on the specified loss. Not a theorem for R43's CE/compare/risk mixture. |
| P8 | Hepburn et al., LIDTA 2018 | Abstract; Sec.2 Eq.1-4; Sec.5.2 | Example-dependent costs can change the proper decision target. Their setting observes cost matrices at decision time, unlike unrun solvers here; do not import their decision rule as an oracle. |

**Prior-adjustment signs and target:** P3 uses post-hoc
`f_j-tau*log(pi_j)` on a conventionally trained classifier, or trains
`CE(f+tau*log(pi),Y)` and predicts with `f`. At the idealized `tau=1` solution
these address balanced classification. Natural-frequency accuracy instead
targets the original posterior; rarity alone is no mandate to suppress its
prior. Two transformations are not an automatic double correction.

**Three different meanings of calibration:** estimating winner probabilities,
obtaining a classification-calibrated surrogate for a specified loss, and
making a good cost-sensitive action are not the same guarantee. NLL/ECE can
improve without Top1 or cost improving. P8's inference-time cost information
cannot be replaced by hidden TEST labels in this project.
These references motivate modeling choices; their guarantees do not transfer
to the R78 mixed objective or macro-cost acceptance gate. In particular, R43's
mixed training does not establish that its maximin scores are calibrated
posterior logits suitable for a plug-in prior correction.
