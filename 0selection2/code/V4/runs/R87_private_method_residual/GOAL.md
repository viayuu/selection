The application research goal remains active and unresolved. This pair tests
private instance-dependent nonlinear scoring capacity, not a frequency quota or
posthoc method bias. It does not redefine success as selecting every method once.

The locked protocol is `protocol_lock.json`. R84A is a historical same-data
reference; R87A is the fresh parameter-count-matched shared-residual control for
R87B. Both retain the complete original model and natural R77 supervision.
Every private method participates wherever it is legal across all 18 tasks.
Neither routes examples by winner nor reads current labels in its forward pass.

R85C's completed evidence motivates further work: its locked VAL Top1 is lower
than R84A's, it rescues only 11 of the fixed target misses while adding 10 false
picks, and well-supported methods remain unselected on TRAIN. These observations
do not identify a unique cause. R85's other committed arms were still running at
this lock and are not judged here. They continue unchanged; no card is preempted.

The proposed residuals may still encode method priors or overfit. More
parameters, a nonzero selection count, a better TRAIN score or fewer strict-zero
groups is not proof of useful recovery. Inspect genuine fixed-target rescues,
lost winners, new false picks, conditional behavior and full-system costs.
The fixed target set is descriptive and never changes training sampling or
checkpoint selection.

from the same fresh prepared seed2 common parameters. Global IDs whose branches
are never legal stay dormant and must not be presented as effective trained
capacity. The shared control has the same nominal parameter count but a
different allocation and effective use; that difference is the tested mechanism.
